Question 1: Primary Characteristics & Computer Generations

List four primary characteristics of a modern digital computer and identify the key hardware technology associated with each of the five generations of computers.

Primary Characteristics of Modern Digital Computers

🚀 Speed

Computers execute millions of instructions per second (MIPS), processing complex arithmetic operations instantly.

🎯 Accuracy

Computers produce flawless, ultra-precise outputs unless provided with faulty data (GIGO principle).

💪 Diligence

Unlike human operators, machines handle repetitive tasks without fatigue, loss of focus, or degradation.

🔄 Versatility

Capable of switching between tasks instantly, from multi-layered simulations to rendering office suites.

The Five Generations of Hardware Evolution

Generation Core Hardware Technology Key Paradigm Shifts
1st Gen (1940-1956) Vacuum Tubes Massive footprints, excessive heat generation, machine language dependencies.
2nd Gen (1956-1963) Transistors Rapid shrinkage in size, reduction in cooling needs, introduction of early Assembly paths.
3rd Gen (1963-1971) Integrated Circuits (ICs) Semiconductor silicon packaging allows processing pipelines to grow exponentially.
4th Gen (1971-Present) Microprocessors (VLSI) Entire CPU frameworks housed on a single microchip silicon node. High scalability.
5th Gen (Present-Beyond) ULSI & AI / Quantum Links Ultra Large-Scale Integration combined with massively parallel neural array architectures.

Question 3: Automated Campus Infrastructure

Demonstrate how optical data scanning devices and electronic card readers can be deployed in a campus environment to automate student attendance and library checkouts.

📌 Attendance Automation via RFID

Students tap their RFID smart cards on scanners positioned at classroom thresholds. The reader extracts the student's unique token and instantly updates the central attendance database via the campus network.

📚 Library Checkouts via Barcodes & RFID

Self-checkout counters read the student's smart ID card and scan the book's asset barcode or RFID tag. The library database cross-references inventory tables, flags the item as checked out, and logs the return due date.

Question 5: Point-and-Draw Devices vs. Graphics Digitizers

Compare point-and-draw devices (such as a mouse and trackball) with graphics digitizers/drawing tablets, highlighting how their coordinate tracking mechanisms and pressure sensitivity differ for CAD and digital art applications.

🖱️ Mouse & Trackball Systems

Tracking Engine: Relative coordinate displacement. Moving the device tracks relative offsets rather than fixed points on the screen.

Pressure Resolution: Binary input options only (click or no click).

Optimal Application: Exceptional for standard geometric positioning and precision CAD point selection workflows.

✍️ Graphics Digitizers & Drawing Tablets

Tracking Engine: Absolute mapping grids. Every position on the tablet surface corresponds directly to an exact coordinate pixel on screen.

Pressure Resolution: Deep multi-level tracking arrays (e.g., 8192 levels of brush weight recognition).

Optimal Application: Essential for natural digital illustration, shading, and variable-line brushwork.

Question 6: Engineering Blueprint System Analysis

Assess whether a drum/flatbed plotter or an ultra-high-definition monitor and large-format printer combination is superior for producing civil engineering blueprints, justifying your selection based on line resolution, scalability, and long-term production costs.

Architectural Choice: The Ultra-High-Definition (UHD) Monitor + Inkjet Large-Format Printer setup is vastly superior to historical plotters for modern production pipelines.

🎯 Line Resolution

Modern wide-format printers easily reach up to 2400 DPI, generating crisp vector text and clean geometric arcs.

📈 Scalability

Easily handles multi-layered schematics, standard PDF documents, and detailed high-density raster image maps.

💰 Production Cost

Shared ink networks, universal part availability, and low maintenance fees offer massive savings over specialized plotters.

👁️ Proofing Speed

UHD monitors enable instant electronic design checks, saving time and reducing physical test-print waste.

Question 7: Biometric Systems in Public Spaces

Critique the operational reliability and security vulnerabilities of vision-input devices (facial recognition) compared to voice recognition devices when deployed as primary biometric authentication tools in high-ambient-noise, publicly exposed environments.

👁️ Facial Recognition Arrays

Reliability: Robust in noisy environments, but susceptible to changing lighting conditions or camera occlusions.

Vulnerabilities: Basic 2D systems can be spoofed by high-res print photos, though modern 3D depth sensors mitigate this risk.

🗣️ Voice Authentication Systems

Reliability: Drops significantly in high-noise environments as ambient interference disrupts pattern matching algorithms.

Vulnerabilities: Highly susceptible to playback attacks using high-quality digital recordings or AI voice clones.
